## What an exclusion is
- An exclusion removes a cause of loss, a kind of property or a situation from coverage.
- Coverage is decided by the insuring agreement and the exclusions together: the promise, then what's carved out of it.

## Why exclusions exist
- Some losses are uninsurable or nearly certain, like wear and tear or deterioration.
- Some are catastrophic and handled by separate policies or programs, like flood.
- Some belong under another kind of policy: a business activity under a business policy, a car under an auto policy.
- Some would encourage bad behavior, like intentional damage.

## Common examples on a homeowners policy
- Flood is commonly excluded and covered by separate flood insurance.
- Earth movement, such as earthquake, is commonly excluded unless added back.
- Wear and tear, neglect, and gradual damage such as a slow leak are commonly excluded.
- Intentional acts by an insured are excluded.

## Endorsements can change the picture
- An endorsement can add back coverage an exclusion took away, usually for more premium.
- An exclusion can also be narrowed or broadened by the policy's definitions, which is why definitions are worth reading too.
