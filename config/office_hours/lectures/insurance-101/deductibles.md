## What a deductible is
- The deductible is the part of a covered loss you pay before the insurer pays.
- Example: a $1,000 deductible on a $6,000 covered loss. The insurer pays $5,000.
- If the loss is smaller than the deductible, the insurer pays nothing for it.

## Why deductibles exist
- They keep small, routine losses out of the pool, where handling them would cost more than they're worth.
- They give the policyholder a reason to prevent losses.
- A higher deductible generally means a lower premium.

## Kinds of deductibles
- A flat-dollar deductible is a set amount per claim.
- A percentage deductible is a share of a limit, often the dwelling limit on a homeowners policy, and it can be much larger than a flat one.
- Some policies have separate deductibles for particular perils, such as wind and hail.
- Health insurance deductibles work differently: they usually apply per year, not per claim.

## Choosing one
- Pick a deductible you could pay tomorrow without trouble.
- Compare the premium savings with how often you'd expect to pay the deductible.
