## What a premium is
- The premium is the price of the policy for the policy period.
- It's paid up front or in installments; installment plans can carry a fee.

## What goes into it
- Expected losses: what the insurer expects to pay in claims for people like you.
- Expenses: running the company, paying agents, handling claims.
- A margin for profit and for the chance that losses run higher than expected.
- Rating factors: characteristics that predict losses, which vary by line of insurance and by state.

## Why premiums change
- Claims costs change: repair costs, medical costs and building materials go up and down.
- Your own claims history and changes to what you insure.
- The pool's experience: if losses in your area rise, premiums tend to follow.
- Many states regulate how insurers file and change rates.

## Lowering it honestly
- A higher deductible usually lowers the premium, because you keep the small losses.
- Ask about discounts and bundling, but compare coverage, not just price.
- A cheaper policy that leaves out a coverage you need isn't cheaper when you have that loss.
